C. INDEPENDENT SPATIAL COHORT: GSE246011 (4 sections, 4,196 spots)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   Not used in the submitted manuscript. Distance to the nearest
   PTPRC-high spot, modelled with a random intercept per section.

   READ THIS BEFORE QUOTING THE COEFFICIENT. This is not a like-for-like
   replication of the primary Visium analysis. Three definitions differ,
   and each of them can move the sign:
     - immune-rich here is the top PTPRC quartile, so a quarter of every
       section is flagged immune by construction; the primary analysis
       uses a deconvolved immune fraction above 0.15, a far sparser set
     - distance here is to the single nearest immune spot (k = 1); the
       primary analysis averages the nearest five
     - exposure here is the raw per-spot CEACAM5 + CEACAM6 sum, with
       epithelial content entered only as a covariate; the primary
       analysis uses the CEACAM-high fraction within epithelium
   With a quarter of the array marked immune, the outcome behaves partly
   as a measure of local spot density, and dense epithelium - which is
   where CEACAM is high - sits close to any spot. The negative sign is
   therefore expected under this parameterisation and is not evidence
   against the primary result. It is also not evidence for it.

      unadjusted               CEACAM beta = -0.3599 [-0.3900, -0.3298]   P = 3.74e-121
      + epithelial content     CEACAM beta = -0.2930 [-0.3342, -0.2518]   P = 3.61e-44

D. IS TOTAL EPITHELIAL CONTENT AN ADMISSIBLE COVARIATE?
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   The deconvolution assigns every spot to epithelium, immune or stroma,
   and the three proportions sum to one. Total epithelial content is
   therefore 1 - immune - stroma: the algebraic complement of the
   quantity that defines both outcomes.

   deconvolution proportions per spot, sum                               1.0000   max deviation from 1 = 1.22e-15
   corr(total epithelial content, immune + stromal content)             -1.0000   arithmetic complement, not an empirical association
   corr(total epithelial content, immune content)                       -0.8908
   corr(total epithelial content, stromal content)                      -0.4686
   corr(total epithelial content, local epithelial density)              0.8644   the two adjustments are near-duplicates of one another
   corr(CEACAM ratio, total epithelial content)                          0.5649   the exposure is a within-epithelium ratio, so this is biological rather than arithmetic
   Distance to immune-rich regions: coefficient on total epithelial content alone    1.7724   P = 0
   Distance to stroma: coefficient on total epithelial content alone     2.0852   P = 0

   The complement correlation is -1 to machine precision, so conditioning
   on epithelial content conditions on the outcome's own determinant. The
   attenuation seen in that model is a property of the parameterisation,
   not evidence against the association. Adjustment for annotated spatial
   domain, which is not a compositional complement, is the admissible
   architecture control and is reported in section A.

CONCLUSION
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   Epithelial density carries 95% of the CEACAM-distance
   association on average. Because CEACAM5/6 are homophilic adhesion
   molecules whose function is to pack epithelium tightly, density is best
   treated as a step on the causal path rather than as a nuisance variable;
   adjusting it away is over-adjustment. We report both analyses and let
   the reader judge.
   The bulk cohort provides the orthogonal, purity-adjusted test the
   Visium data cannot, at n = 45 rather than n = 10.
   GSE246011 runs in the opposite direction, but under a different
   definition of immune-rich, of distance and of the exposure, so it
   estimates a different quantity. It is reported here for
   completeness and is deliberately NOT cited in the manuscript as
   independent replication; see the caveat in section C.
